# Fantasky Master: design notes

The full record behind CLAUDE.md: every feature as built, what the user asked for (their words quoted), what was tried and removed, and measurements. Read the part for an area before changing it, so nothing removed comes back and nothing asked for is undone. Code names here may have moved on; trust the code. It's brought up to date with every PR (on request: "dont wait for milestones anymore for notes"; it had been updated only at milestones).

Fantasy league for the TV show *Taskmaster*. Players pick one contestant per episode and score Show points (the contestant's score) and League points (5-4-3-2-1 by finishing place). Rules: `README.md`. Every calculation: `FANTASKY_MASTER_EXPLAINED.md`.

- **Live app:** https://kbroadie.github.io/fantasky-master/ (GitHub Pages serves `main` as-is). The repo is `kbroadie/fantasky-master`, lower case by convention (renamed on request from `Fantasky-Master`; Pages paths are case-sensitive, and the old addresses weren't kept, as they'd never been shared). The app is the site's root (promoted on request; it used to live in `app/`, and the old single-file site it replaced is only in git history: don't build on it).

## UX framework

The user's v1 prototype is the model: fast, clean, obvious navigation. The aim is to make it fun and emotionally engaging without making anyone think harder.

- **Top bar** (sticky, frosted glass):
  - The brand.
  - The series number, a gold chip with a swap icon: tap it to switch series.
  - One line of text counting down to the next episode: "Ep 5 airs in 5d 18h PDT" (no pill or background; the numbers are gold), ending with the device's time zone at the air time in DM Mono `--t3` (`#cd-tz`, on request: "PDT", "BST", "MESZ" in German, "GMT-7" where a locale has no short name; hidden once the episode is on air), which stays in the compact bar. Under a day it reads hours and minutes ("19h 25m"), under an hour minutes and seconds (`until` in `ui.js`). The air date and time are on the episode page, not here. Compact, it's just the gold time (on request): "Ep 5 airs in" (`#cd-what`) fades where it stood over the first half of the compacting (the time is right-aligned, so it stays put); "Series 22 complete" stays whole.
  - Below them, three segmented tabs, with a gold panel that slides behind the active one. The selected tab is right from the first frame, before the data and fonts load (fixed on request: the Standings icon had shown grey on the gold panel until loading finished): `index.html` marks Standings `aria-selected`, and `main.js` sets the tab and panel from the address before it waits for anything. Their labels are 12px DM Mono (up from 11, on request: they're the most-used buttons).
  - On scroll it compacts to one thin line plus label-only tabs (`--hdr-h`, `--tabs-h`… in `:root`; `--bar-h` is its open height, `--bar-d` what compacting saves, 50px). The bar is `position: fixed` over a spacer of its open height (`main`'s padding-top), so compacting never re-lays out or moves the page (on request: animating the page's layout made it choppy; and if the page moved, scroll anchoring would flip the bar open and shut). **It compacts in step with the scroll** (on request: "what if instead of snapping states the masthead, tab bar, and subtab bar just dynamically scaled with the scroll"; it had snapped to a compact state past 16px and eased there over .3s): over the first `--bar-d` of the page's scroll every part moves in proportion, and scrolling back opens it the same way, so there's no animation of its own to stutter. It's **scroll-driven CSS animations, transforms and opacity only** ("The bar compacts with the scroll" in `styles.css`: `animation-timeline: var(--tl)`, `animation-range: 0 var(--bar-d)`, `bar-*` keyframes), so the compositor runs them while the main thread is busy (Chrome, Safari 26; measured at a 4× throttled CPU over six compact/open cycles: main-thread work 704 → 197ms upright, 1,266 → 449 upside down, and no long frames, from 3 and 5). `--tl` is `scroll(root)`. Where a browser has no scroll-driven animations (Firefox, older Safari), and whenever the page is turned over, `barP` in `main.js` sets `--p` (0–1) on the bar and the strips as the page scrolls, and the rules after the animations follow it (an animation, where there is one, overrides them; checked: the same frames). Turned over, the page scrolls itself on the main thread anyway, and the animations, on `main`'s own timeline, were restyled every frame of every scroll, the corner radii and the tabs' clip repainted (on request: rainbow view scrolling was slow on an iPhone), so there the animations are off and `--p` is written only when it changes: past the first 50px a scroll costs the bar nothing. The tab icons move by `transform`, not `translate`, which Chrome won't run on the compositor for an SVG. The parts: the frosted glass is its own layer (`.topbar::before`) at the open height that slides up by `--bar-d`, so the blur is never re-rendered at a new size; the masthead keeps its open height and slides its background and rule (`.hdr::before`) and its shadow (`.hdr::after`, fading in), the brand rises and scales to .78, and the countdown rises; nothing in the tabs changes size in the layout: the tabs rise 29px (`.tabs`, `translate`), their pill (`.tabs-bg`) squeezes 58 → 40px and the gold panel 46 → 28 (`scale`, from the top), each keeping its round corners (on request: "maintain border radius while resizing": a box squeezed by k has a vertical radius of r / k, a second, main-thread animation at eight points, as a `border-radius` alongside the transforms would put them all on the main thread), the icons fade out over the first 60% as the labels rise 20px over them. The numbers are the old compact layout's, measured, so the open and compact states are the old ones (checked, upright and upside down; the pill's and panel's top and bottom hairlines and shadows are squeezed with them, too slight to see). As the boxes keep their open size, the bar is `pointer-events: none` but for the glass, the masthead and the tabs, and each tab's hit area is clipped as it squeezes (`bar-tab`), so a compact bar never takes the strip's taps. Keep it transform-based.
  - **Hides while scrolling down** (on request, like Safari's address bar): past the first 120px, 12px of scrolling down slides the compact bar up out of sight (`.topbar.hidden`, a transform; `setHidden` in `main.js`) and any 8px back up brings it back; the overscroll bounce at either end is ignored, and it shows again if the keyboard focuses into it. The sticky strip stays, sliding up to the top (its 32px band just under the safe area). Under a notch or status bar a frosted band stays (`body.bar-hidden::before`, `env(safe-area-inset-top)` tall, so nothing in a browser tab).
  - The sub-tab strips (Standings' Ep 1–10, the Episodes strip, the Cast names; `.page > .strip`) are sticky too (on request), right under the bar, and compact with it, in step with the scroll, by transform only: the strip keeps its size (`--strip-h`, 44px) and slides up by `--bar-d` plus 12px (`bar-strip`, a `translate`; hidden, a `transform` on top takes it on to the top), tucking its top under the bar and leaving a `--strip-hc` 32px band, and each tab's label (a `<span>` inside the tab) drops 6px to stay centred in it (checked: the same positions as the old layout-animated version, open and compact, and the page height never changes).   - Standings: an opened half offers the five contestants and None for that player's pick in the week on show (`pickChooser`), replacing the ten-picks strip; the totals and places update at once (every slide is re-rendered by `refresh` in `main.js`, and the opened half stays with the player).
  - Episodes are edited in place, not in a separate form (on request). The title becomes an input set like the heading (`edTitle`, dashed underline; Enter or leaving it applies it). An aired episode's task table becomes inputs (`edTable`: names and scores, 16px so iOS doesn't zoom; the type icon cycles Prize → Filmed → Team → Live; live totals; "+ Task" and Tiebreak below it; a blank table of five tasks before any scores). What's typed is kept in a draft and applied when focus leaves the table (`commit`), so moving between boxes never redraws the page under the keyboard; empty tasks are dropped, and a task with no name or a score that isn't −1–10 or DQ is outlined and not applied. Save applies any draft first, and `refresh` puts focus back on the same box after a redraw (`data-fk`). Under the title a "Get scores from the wiki" button (before broadcast, "Get title from the wiki") fills in the table (`fetchEpisode` in `wiki.js`: the Series page lists each episode's page; its `tmtable` gives each task's type (Prize/Team/Live labels, "Team Live" counts as Live), scores, split-task parts (max of the parent and the parts' sum), bonus rows (added) and tiebreak ✔). The data file's task names and DQs are kept (the wiki often shows a DQ as 0); a placeholder title is filled in too. Before broadcast it fetches only the title, from the Series page's episode list (`fetchTitle` in `wiki.js`; the wiki lists titles before broadcast).
  - Every change is an op replayed onto the file: at once onto the page's copy (the page re-renders via `refresh` in `main.js`), and on Save onto a fresh copy from GitHub, so nobody else's edits are lost (a conflict retries). The result must pass `checkData` (the rules CI runs) before it's sent; warnings (a missing tiebreak) ask first. Rows go where a person would put them: scores right after their episode row, picks at the end of the episode's block in player order. `parseRows`/`toCSV` in `csv.js` round-trip the file byte for byte, so untouched rows never change.
- **Wiki sync** (on request: "1 hour after the YouTube stream starts, automatically sync with the wiki and check future episode titles"): a scheduled GitHub Action, `.github/workflows/wiki-sync.yml`, runs `tools/sync-wiki.mjs` at 22:05 and 23:05 UTC on Thursdays (an hour after the 10pm London stream in summer and winter time) and hourly through Friday, and commits whatever changed to `main` as github-actions[bot] ("Scores: Series 22 Ep 7 (Taskmaster Wiki)"), then asks Pages to rebuild and runs check-data (a push by the workflow's token doesn't start the Checks workflow). The tool:
  - **Scores**: only the next episode to score, only once its stream started at least an hour ago (`airInstant`), and only when the wiki's table looks finished (`complete` from `fetchEpisode` in `wiki.js`: every score in, a total for everyone that the tasks add up to, and a live task); otherwise it leaves it for the next run. A tie for first the wiki has no tiebreak for yet waits too. It writes the same rows edit mode would (`js/ops.js`, the wiki's task names, its title if the episode has a placeholder one); re-syncing Episode 6 from the wiki gave a file byte for byte the same as the hand-entered one. It never touches an episode that already has scores, so the commissioner's corrections and DQs (the wiki often shows a DQ as 0; mark them in edit mode) are never overwritten.
  - **Titles**: every episode still to score whose title is a placeholder ("Episode 9") or differs from the wiki's (`fetchTitles`, one request; differences only of case or punctuation are left alone, so hand-tidied titles stay).
  - Picks aren't on the wiki, so they're still entered in edit mode. The result must pass `checkData` (with the all-time stats, as CI) or nothing is written and the run fails (GitHub emails the repo owner). If edit mode saves while it runs, the push is refused and it starts again from that copy (three tries). Run it by hand from the Actions tab (Run workflow); locally, `node tools/sync-wiki.mjs` (`--now <time>` to pretend it's another time, `FM_CSV=<file>` for another copy).
  - The CSV loads with `cache: "no-cache"` (no preload), so a save shows for everyone once Pages redeploys (about a minute).
- **Link preview and home-screen icon** (on request, for sharing the site in the WhatsApp group): `index.html`'s Open Graph tags give WhatsApp, iMessage and Slack a title, the description and `img/share.jpg` (1200 × 630, ~130 KB: the kicker "A Taskmaster fantasy league", the brand in Bungee and the current series' five framed portraits, on the hero's red stage with the grain). The tags' URLs are absolute (`https://kbroadie.github.io/fantasky-master/…`), as previews need. The icon is "FM" in gold Bungee on the same stage, full bleed with nothing near the edges (iOS rounds the corners, Android may crop to a circle): `img/icon-180.png` is the `apple-touch-icon`, `icon-192`/`icon-512` are in `manifest.webmanifest`, and `icon-32`/`icon-48` (with 192 for sharp screens) are the browser-tab favicon (on request; it replaced a red circle with a "T"). The tool renders with `--disable-lcd-text`, so small icons have no colour fringes. The home-screen name is "Fantasky" (`apple-mobile-web-app-title`, `short_name`). `display` is `browser`, so a home-screen launch opens a normal tab where pull-to-refresh still fetches new scores. All are drawn by `tools/share-images.mjs` (`npm run share`, with `FM_CURL_IMAGES=1` in the sandbox): re-run it when a new series starts, so the preview shows the new cast. WhatsApp caches a link's preview, so a changed image may only show on links shared after the change.
- **Times are local:** every date, time and countdown uses the device's time zone and locale (`fmtDay`, `fmtWhen` in `ui.js`). Never hard-code London. Episodes air at 10pm London time (`AIR_TZ`, `AIR_HOUR`); `airInstant` turns that into an exact instant with daylight saving applied (checked: BST episodes are 21:00 UTC, GMT ones 22:00, so the week between the UK and US clock changes lands an hour later in the US).
- **Icons:** task types (Prize, Filmed, Team, Live) use one monoline SVG set (`ICON_PATHS` / `icon()` in `ui.js`): 16px grid, 1.5 stroke, gold. Don't mix in emoji; they render in clashing styles. Emoji are only for the 👑/🏆/crown badges.
- **Nothing smaller than 11px.** Use weight and colour for hierarchy.
- **Underlines are dotted** (on request): every underlined link or button (`a` by default, "Read the welcome") is `underline dotted 1px` (thin dots, on request), 3px below the text.
- **Standings rows** must not be compositing layers of their own: giving each one `will-change: transform` doubled the layers, and iOS then dropped rows' text after scrolling up and down.
- **Emotion comes from faces, gold and colour, not motion.** Use framed portraits, a crown for the winner, gold for first place, gold for a pick that won, and contestant accent colours. The only decorative motion is the episode podium effects, the drifting light on the scoring cards and Fantastikal Delusion (all requested; see above), which stop under `prefers-reduced-motion`; all of it is at its lowest quality by default, and off but for the podium effects, the Footer and the transition, as frame pacing comes first (on request). Otherwise transitions are only for navigation and touch feedback, such as the tab slide, the row expanding, the week slides, the footer gliding with them and an opened journey drawing in.

## Conventions

- **No personalisation:** there is no "you", player picker or planner (removed on request). There are three tabs only.
- **No build step, no framework, no runtime dependencies.** Native ES modules; `package.json` is for tooling only.
- **Views** are functions returning template strings. Escape user and CSV text with `esc()`, or `rich()` to allow `<strong>`. Interactions go through delegated listeners in `main.js`.
- **Contestants** always appear as full gold-framed portraits (`framed()`), never cropped circles.
- **Fonts:** the v1 set (with Nunito for names in place of Fredoka), loaded from Google Fonts, one job each:
  - `--ff-head` Bungee: headings and the brand. It has one weight; keep it at 400.
  - `--ff-name` Nunito: player and contestant names, at 800 (700 for the edit-mode chooser). Chosen on request for legibility over the v1 Fredoka, whose wide, heavy rounded letters were hard to read at 16px; Nunito keeps the rounded, friendly feel with ordinary letter shapes (Lexend, Figtree, Atkinson Hyperlegible, Outfit, Rubik, DM Sans and Inter were compared on the Standings board). Only 700 and 800 are loaded.
  - `--ff` Inter: body text and every score.
  - `--fm` DM Mono: labels. Only 400 and 500 are loaded.
  - `font-synthesis: none` is set, so never ask for a weight that isn't loaded.
  - The first render waits for the faces it uses (`FACES` in `main.js`), loaded alongside the data and given up after 1.2s, so the page isn't drawn in fallbacks and laid out again when they swap in. Add a face there when a new weight is used.
  - Chewy and Cinzel are Patatas's alone (on request: "Choose more thematic fonts for patatas"): Chewy for his speech, Cinzel 700 for his name plate, both loaded for just the letters he uses (`text=` on their own Google Fonts request, a few KB) and in `FACES`. Pacifico, once "Fantasy" in the brand, is gone.
  - Use tabular figures for numbers.
- **Terms:** use **Show** and **League** points, as in README.md.
- **Images live in the repo** (`img/s21/`, `img/s22/`), served by GitHub Pages with the site: Imgur is blocked in the UK, so nothing loads from it (moved on request). Each contestant has a portrait, `img/s22/chloe-portrait.webp` (225 × 266 WebP, ~37 KB, the CSV's `portrait_url`; `check-data` checks the file exists), and a profile photo, `img/s22/chloe.webp` (`heroes.js`). Patatas is `img/patatas.webp` (271 × 320, ~43 KB), set into the same frame as the portraits.
- **Profile faces** (`heroes.js`): each is a WebP of about 1,000px cropped to just the part of the photo the Profile card can show, ~70–200 KB (compressed on request: the full photos were 5.2 MB for ten, now 1.15 MB). The card shows a window around the face: the photo is drawn ~1,000–1,500px wide (the head `--face` across), so it can't be shrunk without going soft on a 3× iPhone, only cropped. The crop is the card's area in the photo at every width from 320 to 1920px in both series, plus 4% of the photo on each side, and re-measured pixel for pixel against the uncropped photos (identical but for compression noise); `eye`, `head` and `ratio` are fractions of the crop. Series 21's come from each contestant's own hero photo, Series 22's from the cast's group photo (a 20.6-megapixel photo drawn whole came out ~3,200px wide and iPhone Safari never drew it). If the Profile card's layout or `--face` changes, re-check that no photo's edge shows. Crop any new photo the same way.
